Background

As a participant in the Irvine Statistical Institute for Biostatistics and Undergraduate Data Science (ISI-BUDS) summer research program, I had the opportunity to work alongside Dr. Volodymyr Minin, Dr. Sanghyuk Shin, and Jessalyn Sebastian, as well as three other undergraduate students, on a project examining the impact of universal Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) policies on the effect of HIV on tuberculosis (TB) treatment outcomes in Botswana. In particular, we collaborated with medical professionals in Gaborone, Botswana to analyze two cohorts of TB patients, one that preceeded the policy and another that followed it, to determine the extent to which HIV remains a risk factor for unfavorable TB outcomes. Our continuing analysis takes into account many confounders commonly associated with TB outcomes and considers spaciotemporal heterogenity in the data to obtain robust conclusions. Additionally, we are working to extend our study to compare the situation in Gaborone to places of similar HIV prevalence to obtain a Bayesian meta-analysis in lieu of a available data. Our future directions include employing more complex models that better suited for some of the nonlinear relationships that we identify in our data.
