Background

The Kaczmarz method is an iterative algorithm that falls under the category of "sketch and project" methods for solving linear systems. The method was famously applied under the name of the "algebraic reconstruction technique" (ART) for solving large linear systems in the context of computerized tomography (CT). More recently popularized by the mathematicians Strohmer and Vershynin (2009), two researchers close to my academic circle, a family of randomized Kaczmarz methods promote much faster convergence in expectation than the original.

I was invited by Dr. Anna Ma to work alongside her and two other undergraduates to explore different sampling alternatives to further improve existing algorithms in the space. Our work culminated in a paper that we recently submitted to ArXiv, comprised of our research over the Fall 2024-Spring 2025 academic year. Looking to target inefficiencies in existing methods at addressing compormised performance on ill-conditioned linear systems, our work explores the a variety of extensions to obtain faster convergence. Our results highlight the potential of utilizing row differences, clustering methods, and spectral properties of the linear system to obtain twice faster convergence than methods we examined.
